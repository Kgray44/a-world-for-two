# Rosewater audience chamber: ivory ribs and inhabited edges

The actual candidate 37 shader view exposed a broad grey room under a flat dark
ceiling. Its existing paired thrones, chandelier, glazing and furnishings provide
a useful starting point, but do not by themselves give the room a composed
interior. This separate R4 proposal concentrates on those visible weaknesses.

The audience chamber is inside the royal keep's octagonal footprint, approximately
X 1256–1275 and Z −1041–−1022. Its main floor is Y 72 and the retained floor above
is Y 82. No palace wing, exterior footprint, room, roof or stair is added.

The design adds a shallow coffered soffit with distinct timber cross members,
carved construction joints and recessed ivory panels. Two shaped limestone
arches frame the throne setting and arrival, with engaged pendant springers
connected to actual masonry backing. The original chandelier hangs between them;
its chain, mount and light remain intact. This changes the room's section and
shadow pattern while keeping its upper floor untouched.

Actual Macaw tied rose curtains and golden rods provide a richer textile setting
beside the thrones. Corbelled shelves carry stable potted flowering shrubs rather
than an active tree beside the architecture. Low cherry cupboards, visible books,
a candlelit side desk and a laid refreshment corner make the walls feel used.
The original seats and their access, banners, windows, lamps, raised dais, main
runner, western rotunda threshold and eastern gallery connection are fixed.

`tools/artistic_castle_interior.py` pins the exact current authored palace master
and reads actual block predecessors from a separately identified verified backup.
Every new target must be inside the reviewed room, have an authored predecessor
and match the saved block identity and supplied orientation. Existing saved block
entities, fluids or unrelated changes refuse placement admission. All changes are
listed with exact old/new states. The proposal loads no block-entity NBT, creates
no entities, supplies no inventories and emits no commands.

The complete composed palace, including all earlier floors and roofs, must pass
the owner's material rule: no uninterrupted material run above nine blocks, and
no material above three blocks in more than one Cartesian axis. Facing changes
do not count as different materials. Timber members meet designed joints and
separate ivory fields; the validator does not recolor the building to make a test
pass. These geometry checks do not establish beauty.

The six focused regressions cover the complete-building rule, native property
validation, preserved chandelier/glazing/thrones, protected saved block entities,
foreign predecessor refusal, ceiling and springer support, unchanged circulation,
and exact command-free delta/preimage custody. Their strict block-property fixture
contains only actual pinned native schemas and a synthetic world identity. It
includes the original palace builder's transient window input; a missing schema
is an error rather than a permissive fallback.
