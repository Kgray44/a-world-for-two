# Original age-of-sail ships

The harbor vessels are original Minecraft constructions inspired by historical
organization and proportions. They are not reproductions of a licensed schematic
or a museum ship. Structural flagship/merchant/corsair builds and usable Small
Ships vessels have different roles and separate functionality checks.

Royal Museums Greenwich's [Liverpool (1758) plan, ZAZ3359](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-83150)
records upper and lower decks and fore/aft platforms for a 28-gun sixth-rate
frigate. It informs distinct deck functions and raised end platforms.

The [USS Constitution Museum's deck study](https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/2016/07/14/decks/)
describes the spar, gun, berth and orlop decks and structural knees and deck beams.
Those distinctions inform the larger naval vessel's circulation and internal
framing. Its published modern restoration drawings are not treated as exact
eighteenth-century room layouts.

The [U.S. Navy's Constitution fact file](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169652/uss-constitution/uss-constitution/)
describes a three-masted square rig, a covered gun deck, and boats used for ship-to-
shore transport. These are useful references for rig silhouettes and tender access.

The Minecraft interpretation provides walkable deck heights and broadside access
lanes. Hull sections must actually contain the rooms. Cabins, crew berths, mess,
galley, stores, sail/rope lockers and a separate magazine receive different layouts
according to each vessel's role. Magazines use safe storage and decoration; guns
remain unloaded. No automatic combat or persistent ship physics is required for
the four large architectural vessels.

Actual ocean depth, preserved structures, safe boarding, hatch continuity,
interior dryness, lighting, native block orientation, restart persistence and
shader screenshots are acceptance tasks. A generated hull plan proves none of
those by itself.
