# Rosewater Lark: one artistic recovery prototype

**Status: R1 and R2 were genuinely photographed. R2 improved the hull/interiors
but still failed the deck-composition review. R3 awaits its own native graphical
review.** This is one replacement design for an isolated staging world.
It is not an accepted fleet template or permission to rebuild the permanent world.

The owner's two critical recovery addenda reject the previous architectural result.
Successful placement and old block counts do not reverse that judgment. The latest
owner direction permits starting over without retaining tonight's world/player
changes; the host still owns world selection, backups and deployment. This tool
does not reset or connect to any world.

## What the genuine baseline shows

The host captured `before-a/ship-broadside.png` and `ship-quarter.png` in the actual
installed graphical client with Reimagined enabled. Both were visually inspected.
The broadside has a nearly straight upper hull and very little visible sheer.
The elevated view exposes a blunt, broad bow and a large uninterrupted tan deck.
Thick zigzag diagonal rigging dominates the silhouette. The large rectangular
weather deck reads as a platform even though rooms and mechanisms exist below it.
These are failures of proportion and composition. More lanterns would not repair
them. The private captures are retained by the host; no screenshot is synthesized
or reproduced as proof of the new vessel.

## What the first real prototype review found

All eight R1 comparison poses were captured in the actual installed client.
The bow, stern, deck, great cabin, gun deck and hold were inspected. The new hull
entry and sheer read more clearly, but the cream frame stations produced an
unwanted vertical barcode. The rigging was still busy and fragmented. Most
seriously, the deck photograph shows actual dropped rope items. This is a block
survival failure, not a texture or distant aliasing problem.

The cabin's furniture was too sparse and its window sightline was obstructed.
The correct native cannon models did not make the gun deck feel worked or lived
in. The hold still read as a long dark empty corridor with cargo in isolated
corners. R1 is retained unchanged as evidence of those shortcomings.

R2 softens the frame joints with related installed Chipped timber finishes and
uses continuous native glass in the small glazed bays. It adds dunnage-framed
casks, merchant crates, folded canvas and repair timber; a sailmaker's workbench;
cooper's tools; a laid mess; personal lockers; ten separate gun-service bays;
galley crockery and pantry; and a chart-table group with books, hourglass, globe,
cupboard and window bench. This is an authored response to specific photographs,
not an artistic pass awarded by counting the added blocks.

R1 mixed two rope families. Inspection of the exact pinned JAR bytecode showed
that Farm & Charm searches its own rope/fixed-anchor graph, with a 256-node bound,
while Supplementaries uses its own rope class or suitable support faces. R2 uses
one rope family, real native knot fittings only on horizontal lashings, shorter
hanging falls, reinforced timber fairleads and fewer lines. The unsupported gun
carriage fragments are removed. Lamps receive actual short chain hangers to
their deck beams. The source attachment check is conservative but does not prove
native survival: the host must let block updates settle, inspect dropped items
and compare the same eight actual views before accepting this revision.

The host subsequently checked all 2,054 retained R2 Farm & Charm rope positions
in the actual settled world, with zero native identity/orientation failures
(terminal `a575a77a-6f6c-40ef-b3bb-3186a26c6e79`). The pickups visible in the R2
photograph can therefore be retained R1 items; that image alone is not proof
of new R2 decay. This narrow result does not award artistic, physics or hoist
acceptance. The supplemental captain photograph reveals the improved table,
seating and window arrangement more clearly than the comparison pose obscured
by the real mizzen post.

## R3: deck work, circulation and quieter rigging

The R2 deck still looked like a large empty platform. R3 gives it distinct working
groups: a waist capstan with a native crank/cog, bitts, three belaying stations,
two pump/maintenance groups, a sail-repair bench, cargo awaiting a lift, a larger
coamed panelled hatch, a glazed companion hood and the longboat's cradle,
thwarts, stowed oars and emergency chest. The quarterdeck has a small instrument
group, and the cabin opens through paired doors onto a two-deep stern balcony.
These native mechanisms remain uncommissioned; a visible crank is not proof of
anchor hauling, pumping or boat lifting.

The isolated emergency-ladder backing is moved away from the main lane and ends
flush with the weather deck. The entire waist lane at local X3/X4,
Z-21..23 is checked two-wide and two-high. The companion stair clearance is
checked after fitting the hood. A low sheer-cap bar that previously crossed the
aft cabin's headroom is removed rather than hidden with a camera angle.

R3 retains the family that survived R2's native check but reduces its rigging to
1,349 positions by stowing long lower running sheets and removing redundant
upper stays/lifts. It uses no new rope model. Whether the simpler silhouette and
fuller working deck actually succeed must be judged from the real R3 views.

## Historical references and original interpretation

The [Royal Museums Greenwich Liverpool body/sheer/half-breadth plan, ZAZ3355](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-83146)
is a 1756 proposal for a 28-gun sixth-rate frigate. It informs the relationship
between a fine entry, fuller middle and narrowed stern. The accompanying
[upper-deck plan, ZAZ3358](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-83149)
and [upper/lower deck and platform plan, ZAZ3359](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-83150)
inform distinct deck functions and raised end platforms. These catalogued plans
belong to different proposal dates; this build does not claim to reproduce an
exact historical ship. No museum image, schematic or artwork is bundled.

The [USS Constitution Museum's deck study](https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/2016/07/14/decks/)
explains the different spar, gun, berth and lower-deck roles and the importance of
deck beams and structural knees. That later, larger frigate is a comparison for
interior organization, rather than an interchangeable Liverpool plan.

The Minecraft hull stations are original authored coordinates. They are not a
trace of the museum drawings. Player clearance and usable furnishings require
some spatial exaggeration. Native screenshots must decide whether that compromise
still looks convincing.

## Intended composition

One approximately 89-by-23-block hull has a fine raked stem, rounded bilges,
tumblehome and a visibly rising sheer. The transom narrows to five window bays,
with small wing galleries and restrained gilding. A short forecastle, open waist
and raised quarterdeck create three clear deck masses. A glazed great cabin
occupies the stern beneath the quarterdeck.

At the proposed water-level origin `(1140,63,-1450)`, the first design occupies
`X1122..1159, Y55..131, Z-1517..-1402`. This includes sparse yards, bowsprit,
channels, tackle and the boarding stair. The hull is much narrower than the full
rig envelope. The keel stays at Y55; the host must still inspect actual seabed
clearance before applying the staging plan. Terrain is not excavated by this tool.

The weather deck has distinct working focal areas: forward windlass/bitts/bell,
framed cargo hatch, switchback companionway, boat skids and a small stowed
longboat, isolated boat-handling tackle, and the raised helm. The three tapered,
slightly raked masts have trestletrees, caps, shrouds, ratlines and restrained
running braces. Furled bundles differ from hanging drying canvas. The main loose
cloth has curved bellies, raised clews, gores, reef bands and one named repair
patch. This is a proposal for visual inspection, not a claim that it looks good.

## Interior itinerary

The main route descends a real two-wide switchback companionway. Separate stair
flights use different lanes, with explicit landings, attached support and two
blocks of headroom. A second attached ladder has reinforced hatch sections.

The great cabin combines a private berth, books, writing desk, chart table,
model ship, upholstered seating and personal stores. Below it, the quieter
navigation office leads toward the covered broadside deck. Twenty-four unloaded
structural guns occupy that deck; four light pieces sit on the quarterdeck.
None is assembled, loaded or wired to fire.

The crew deck has ten anchored two-part hammocks, individual casks, a communal
mess table and a separate after wardroom with two officer berths. The forward
galley has actual Farmer's Delight cabinets, cold stove, cooking pot and prep
board. The deeper hold has themed cargo, fresh-water/provision stores, a sail
locker and carpenter's chest. A full bulkhead separates the magazine's empty
display casks. No TNT, live powder or dangerous firing setup is included.

Sixteen named anchors, including the stern balcony, are checked for planned
reachability. The checker never
carves furniture away to manufacture a passing result. It uses conservative full
block support and cannot prove native stair, rope, furniture or collision shapes.

## Materials and construction rhythm

The owner's rule applies to the complete vessel: no uninterrupted run may exceed
nine blocks, and at most one axis may exceed three blocks for any block material.
Block facing is not a material change. The shared `artistic_materials` checker
runs against the entire final geometry.

The design uses a deliberate construction rhythm: fore/aft plank strakes,
staggered short panels, transverse frame stations, contrasting deck beams and
joint collars. Mast, yard and fore/aft spar materials follow their roles rather
than random colors. Canvas gores and reef bands use the installed Chipped fabric
textures; they are not random white/gray noise. Standing/running rope, visible
splice lashings and short iron tackle fittings are distinct. Reinforced hatch
ladder sections interrupt the continuous wooden ladder. These choices pass the
geometry rule, but their visual density and texture still require actual review.

## Bounded staging workflow

`tools/artistic_ship.py` uses only native state-validation and serialization
helpers from the earlier settlement module. It does not reuse the rejected ship
geometry. `build()` accepts an integer origin and quarter-turn rotation and is
pure in memory. Native property names and values fail closed.

`prepare()` requires an existing world under an explicitly owned artistic staging
home, a matching `artistic-owner.json`, a verified backup receipt, and an actual
native registry naming that exact world. It writes a unique content-addressed
folder and refuses to overwrite an earlier prototype. It never contacts the
server, replaces a generic `plan.json`, installs a mod, or deploys a structure.

The host runs the storage reserve guard before generating private plans. The
bounded output allocation is 32 MiB on the destination drive; the native plan
itself is limited to 16 MiB and 180,000 authored positions. The reserve monitor
does not constrain unrelated applications or writers.

For a revision, `--previous-plan` and its exact `--previous-sha256` produce a new
unique full plan plus `expected-before.json` and `visual-delta.json`. The previous
bundle is never overwritten. The combined serialized size is measured in memory
before writing and must fit the 32 MiB bound. The delta accounts explicitly for
removed old rigging positions and new positions that require an actual-air
check. The manifest also records the finite former unstable-rope scope. R1 rope
decay can make the strict original before-plan fail; the host must capture and
reconcile those actual prior states without silently allowing another mismatch.

Example, after the host's fresh-world registry is ready:

```powershell
python -m tools.artistic_ship `
  --home "D:\A World for Two\staging\world-tests\20261027T053650Z-artistic" `
  --staging-world "D:\A World for Two\staging\world-tests\20261027T053650Z-artistic\world\RosewaterArtistic" `
  --origin 1140 63 -1450
```

The manifest includes transformed camera positions, eye-level interior poses,
yaw/pitch, deck levels, room purposes and pending checks. The native plan names
only the isolated staging world and contains no entity summons. Explicit NBT
tag counts are not a count of every native block entity created by the chosen
blocks.
