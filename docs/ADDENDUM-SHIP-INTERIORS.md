# A WORLD FOR TWO
## Age-of-Sail Ship Interiors & Belowdecks Detail Addendum

**Repository:** https://github.com/Kgray44/a-world-for-two

**Priority:** HIGH — Apply this to the ongoing harbor / pirate-port / ship construction before final acceptance.

This addendum strengthens the requirements for the large wooden sailing ships already requested.

## CORE DIRECTIVE

The large wooden ships must **not** be impressive only from the outside.

I want their **interiors and belowdecks to be just as intricate, believable, detailed, and true-to-life as the exterior hulls, masts, sails, and rigging.**

These ships should feel like real, explorable, age-of-sail vessels from the 17th–18th century.

They should not be hollow decorative shells.

They should feel like actual ships people lived and worked on.

---

## 1. INTERIOR AUTHENTICITY REQUIREMENT

Design the interiors based on the overall logic and spatial organization of real historical wooden sailing ships.

The exact ship classes may vary, but the internal arrangement should be plausibly inspired by real period vessels.

The goal is not perfect museum-grade naval archaeology, but a **convincing, richly detailed, historically inspired ship interior**.

Each major ship should have a believable internal layout with real functional spaces.

Avoid fantasy layouts, giant empty chambers, or arbitrary rooms that break the authenticity of the ship.

---

## 2. REQUIRED SPACES FOR LARGE SHIPS

For the major ships, include appropriate versions of the following spaces where the ship’s size permits.

### Command and officer spaces
- Captain’s cabin / captain’s quarters
- Lieutenant or officer quarters
- Quarterdeck access
- Navigation / chart table area
- Writing desk or planning area
- Stern windows / stern gallery atmosphere where appropriate
- Officer storage / chests / furnishings
- Appropriate decorative prestige for senior spaces

### Crew and living spaces
- Mess deck or crew eating area
- Crew berthing / hammocks / sleeping arrangements
- Basic personal storage
- Shared living areas
- Galley / shipboard cooking area where appropriate
- Barrels, provisions, and shipboard daily-life details

### Combat spaces
- Real cannon deck
- Proper broadside cannon arrangement where appropriate
- Gun access lanes and movement space
- Ammunition storage areas near gun deck where plausible
- Visible naval-combat atmosphere and detailing

### Storage / logistics spaces
- Main hold / cargo hold
- Supply hold
- Food stores
- Rope / sail / maintenance storage
- Powder hold / magazine
- Tool and repair storage
- Spare equipment areas
- Ballast / lower storage logic where appropriate

### Functional circulation
- Believable stairways / ladders between decks
- Hatch access between levels
- Clear movement routes through the ship
- Distinct deck identities
- Logical transitions from upper deck to lower deck to hold

---

## 3. POWDER HOLD / MAGAZINE REQUIREMENT

The powder hold / powder magazine is specifically important.

Include a dedicated, believable belowdecks space for powder storage where appropriate.

It should feel:

- Deliberate
- Dangerous
- Protected
- Separate from general cargo
- Historically inspired in placement and design

Use safe decorative treatment in gameplay terms.

Do not create actual destructive hazards that are likely to ruin the settlement unintentionally.

The powder hold should be visually authentic and atmospheric without becoming a server griefing accident waiting to happen.

---

## 4. EACH LARGE SHIP SHOULD FEEL DISTINCT

Do not simply repeat the same interior layout in every ship.

Different ships should feel like they serve slightly different roles or have different personalities.

Examples:

- A more prestigious flagship
- A rougher pirate or corsair vessel
- A more cargo-oriented ship
- A more heavily armed ship
- A smaller but still elegant coastal trader or escort ship

Different ships may vary in:

- Cabin richness
- Cargo emphasis
- Number and layout of cannons
- Officer accommodations
- Crew-space density
- Deck proportions
- Hold arrangement
- Decorative style
- Wear and weathering
- Amount of clutter and operational detail

The offshore moored ship especially should feel important and impressive.

---

## 5. INTERIOR DETAIL QUALITY

All ship interiors should receive the same level of detail emphasis as the rest of the cinematic settlement.

Use meaningful period-feeling details such as:

- Lanterns and candlelit spaces
- Charts and navigation tables
- Barrels and crates
- Rope storage
- Hammocks or berths
- Cannon-access clearances
- Officer furniture
- Captains’ writing desks
- Dining or planning tables
- Galley details
- Ship tools and maintenance supplies
- Ammunition storage
- Treasure or locked chests where appropriate
- Decorative stern spaces
- Support beams and deck framing
- Convincing ceiling height changes
- Interesting but believable clutter

Avoid large empty decks with only a few props.

Avoid decorative spam that makes navigation impossible.

The goal is **authentic richness**, not random clutter.

---

## 6. MAKE THE SHIPS EXPORABLE AND MEMORABLE

Players should be able to board and meaningfully explore these ships.

Provide:

- Logical entrances / gangways
- Deck-to-deck navigation
- Interesting places to discover
- Great views from upper decks
- Atmospheric belowdecks spaces
- Hidden storage or secrets where appropriate
- Distinctive captain’s cabins
- A satisfying sense of descending deeper into the vessel

The belowdecks experience should feel special.

Walking below the main deck should create a real mood shift: darker, woodier, more enclosed, more atmospheric, and more immersive.

---

## 7. USE SHIPS AS EXPERIENCES, NOT JUST DECORATION

These ships should contain fun points of interest.

Possible features include:

- A captain’s log or note area
- Hidden smuggler stash
- Treasure chest
- Ammunition room
- Captain’s private map room
- Brig or confinement area if appropriate
- Distinctive galley
- Signal or lookout details
- Cargo themes
- Storytelling props
- A skiff-hoist station that feels operational
- Officer meeting room or wardroom atmosphere where fitting

The ships should reward players for actually going aboard and exploring.

---

## 8. TRUE-TO-LIFE EXTERIOR / INTERIOR RELATIONSHIP

Ensure the outer form of each ship supports the internal layout.

Do not create impossible interior volumes that obviously do not fit inside the hull.

Avoid ships that look narrow outside but contain giant interior rooms.

Think about:

- Hull beam
- Deck height
- Stern height
- Mast placement
- Hatch placement
- Cannon position
- Structural believability

The ship should make sense as a whole object.

Interior and exterior should feel like one coherent vessel.

---

## 9. SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS FOR THE OFFSHORE SHIP

The large offshore ship should be especially memorable.

It should be one of the crown jewels of the port.

Requirements:

- Highly detailed exterior
- Strong silhouette
- Partially unfurled drying sails
- A believable tender/skiff transfer concept
- Hoist/tackle system for hauling a skiff aboard
- Fully explorable deck and belowdecks
- Rich captain/officer area
- Good cannon layout
- Strong cargo / hold detail
- Excellent nighttime and sunset appearance

This ship should feel like an iconic cinematic setpiece.

---

## 10. DOCKED SHIPS MUST ALSO BE FULLY REALIZED

The three major dockside ships must also receive serious interior attention.

Do not make the offshore ship detailed and the docked ships shallow.

At least the major docked ships should include meaningful and believable interior deck structure, not just surface-only detailing.

The port should feel like a living harbor with multiple ships worth boarding.

---

## 11. SMALLER SHIPS AND BOATS

Smaller vessels do not need the same degree of complexity, but should still feel thoughtfully built.

Small craft should include believable features such as:

- Seating or rowing positions
- Cargo space
- Fishing gear
- Mooring detail
- Oars or storage suggestion
- Useful access between dock and ship

Their scale should complement the major ships and make the harbor feel active.

---

## 12. VISUAL / CINEMATIC IMPORTANCE

These ships are major visual setpieces.

They should look beautiful:

- From the docks
- From the harbor district
- From the water
- From the castle / higher ground
- In aerial flyovers
- At sunset
- At night with lanterns
- In stormy weather

The interiors should also be visually compelling.

Create beautiful cinematic moments such as:

- Lantern-lit captain’s cabin
- Rows of cannons in low light
- Warm officer quarters
- Dark powder hold
- Cargo-filled lower decks
- Sunlight filtering across upper decks
- A skiff hoist scene near the offshore ship

If practical, include these ships prominently in the cinematic settlement flyover.

---

## 13. PERFORMANCE / SAFETY

Continue using safe bounded building operations.

Preserve already completed harbor and settlement work.

Keep interiors structural and richly built, but avoid unnecessary heavy simulation.

Requirements:

- Minimal ticking burden
- Safe player navigation
- No accidental dangerous explosive setups
- No excessive entity spam
- Clear and stable access routes
- Good lighting without overloading performance
- Backup/rollback protection before major ship revisions

---

## 14. FINAL ACCEPTANCE FOR THIS ADDENDUM

This addendum is complete only when:

- The large ships have genuine, detailed interiors
- The belowdecks are meaningfully built out
- Captains’ / officers’ quarters exist and feel authentic
- Mess deck / living spaces exist
- Cannon deck exists where appropriate
- Cargo hold exists
- Powder hold / magazine exists
- Deck-to-deck circulation is believable
- Ship interiors and exteriors feel coherent together
- The interiors are highly detailed and rewarding to explore
- The offshore and docked ships all feel like real age-of-sail vessels rather than decorative shells

## FINAL DIRECTIVE

I want these ships to feel like **real historic wooden sailing ships brought to life in Minecraft**.

They should be dramatic from the outside and deeply immersive on the inside.

I want players to board them and immediately feel that care was put into every deck — from the captain’s cabin down to the powder hold.

Continue the harbor build and upgrade the ships accordingly.
